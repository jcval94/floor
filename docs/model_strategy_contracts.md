# Model and strategy contracts

FLOOR separates **forecast semantics**, **directional alpha**, **risk geometry**,
**strategy evidence** and **execution policy**. New models or strategies must
declare which role they implement instead of reusing an existing field with a
different meaning.

## Model contract

The registry is `config/model_contracts.json`. Newly trained artifacts must
include a `model_contract` generated by `contracts.model_contract`.

A model contract declares:

- task and horizon;
- semantic role;
- whether the model is directional;
- parameter schema version;
- mandatory parameters;
- serving outputs.

Unknown declared contracts fail closed. Pre-contract champions remain readable
only as a migration path and keep their legacy semantics.

### Classic D1/W1/Q1 ranges

Classic range models have two distinct geometries:

- **central geometry**: typical conditional floor/ceiling used to describe the
  expected range and to define central targets;
- **risk geometry**: wider calibrated one-sided boundaries used for stops and
  reward/risk.

New classic artifacts must emit `params.risk_geometry`. The current contract
calibrates additive downside/upside delta residuals on the earlier chronological
validation block and evaluates them on the later block. The split is made by
whole observation dates, and calibration labels whose target end crosses the
evaluation boundary are purged. The target is 80% marginal coverage per side.

Never label the central classic boundaries as q10/q90 unless a future model
actually trains and validates those quantiles. `PredictionRecord` therefore
leaves `floor_quantile` and `ceiling_quantile` unset by default.

### Adding a future model

1. Add or extend its task in `config/model_contracts.json`.
2. Give the model a truthful semantic role and output mapping.
3. Attach the contract during training with `attach_model_contract`.
4. Add structural validation for its serialized parameters.
5. Keep train/serve feature and parameter parity.
6. Use chronological/purged tuning and a separate calibration/evaluation
   segment when calibration is required.
7. Add regression tests proving contract rejection for malformed artifacts.
8. Do not expose a directional output unless directional lift is evaluated as
   such; range geometry is not directional alpha.
9. Do not promote or serve an artifact whose declared contract fails.

## Strategy contract

The registry is `config/strategy_contracts.json`. Every active strategy and
every Strategy League strategy member must have an explicit semantic contract.

The contract distinguishes:

- research action space;
- the variant evaluated in Strategy League;
- SELL semantics in the League;
- the canonical strategy variant;
- whether League evidence is allowed to support promotion of that canonical
  variant.

The current directional strategies are evaluated by Strategy League as
`long_only_projection`. Their SELL signals mean exit/no position, so that
evidence **cannot** promote the full bidirectional variant. The
`capital_allocation_challenger` is natively long-only and has its own
league-only contract.

## Costs and risk

`config/costs.yaml` is the canonical transaction-cost source.
`config/risk.yaml` is the authoritative operational risk ceiling.

Strategy-local limits may be stricter but cannot enlarge operational limits.
Strategy League keeps its frozen experiment allocation limits, but its execution
cost profile is validated against the canonical shadow-cost contract.

Changing a frozen Strategy League experiment still requires a new league epoch;
contract validation must never rewrite historical evidence.

New Strategy League epochs use a v2 evidence contract that freezes the hashes of
all five serving champions plus the model/strategy contract registries. Legacy
epochs remain readable and continue accumulating their existing history, but are
reported as `legacy_v1_model_suite_unfrozen` and cannot satisfy promotion review.
This avoids retroactively pretending that historical strategy evidence was
generated under a model suite that was never frozen.

## Promotion invariant

A model/strategy can be technically valid without being promotion-ready.
Contracts establish meaning and compatibility; out-of-time/prospective evidence
establishes performance. Passing one never substitutes for the other.
